WHAT WAS ATTEMPTED THIS SESSION AND THE OUTCOME OF EACH CHANGE
---------------------------------------------------------------

1. COMPUTE→COMPUTE FORWARDING IN cast256_enc  [BROKE TIMING - MUST REVERT]

   What it is:
     Instead of spending 1 ADDR cycle per step to drive BRAM addresses from
     registered beta_reg, the COMPUTE state simultaneously applies the
     f-function AND drives BRAM addresses for the NEXT step using a forwarded
     value: d_v = beta_reg.x XOR f_result_comb. This eliminates the ADDR
     cycle between consecutive steps, halving CAST-256 latency from ~98 to
     ~50 cycles/block.

   Why it breaks timing:
     The combinatorial path within one COMPUTE cycle is:
       BRAM_out → DSP(f_result) → XOR(d_v) → CARRY4(km ± d_v) → BRAM addr setup
     Logic alone is ~8 ns. Routing on the congested xc7z010 (>80% LUT
     utilisation with 3 channels) adds 5–7 ns, pushing the total to 19.4 ns
     post-place-and-route. Fmax = 51.5 MHz. Timing violated by 9.4 ns.

   Why it cannot be fixed without adding a full extra cycle:
     The BRAM has 1-cycle registered read latency. For the output of step N+1
     to be available in cycle N+1, the BRAM address for step N+1 must be
     driven combinatorially WITHIN cycle N. That combinatorial path
     (DSP → XOR → CARRY4 → BRAM addr) is the one that is too long.
     Registering any intermediate value (e.g. f_result_comb or d_v) and
     using it in the next cycle to compute the address means the BRAM output
     for step N+1 is only available in cycle N+2 - i.e. 2 cycles per step,
     same latency as ADDR→COMPUTE. There is no way to get 1 cycle per step
     AND break the timing path on this device at this congestion level.

   What to do:
     Revert cast256_enc.vhd to ADDR→COMPUTE. Two specific changes:

     a) addr_comb process:
        Remove the entire "elsif state = COMPUTE then ... end if" block.
        Keep only the "if state = ADDR then ..." block unchanged.
        Remove f_result_comb from the sensitivity list of addr_comb.

     b) main_fsm COMPUTE state:
        Change both "state <= COMPUTE" (the two non-terminal step
        transitions at the bottom of the COMPUTE case) back to
        "state <= ADDR". The terminal transition
        (counter=11 and f_step=3 → ST_DONE) stays as-is.

     c) Update the header comment: latency ~98 cycles/block, remove
        the COMPUTE→COMPUTE forwarding description.

     The addr_comb ADDR branch, the bram_read process, the f_result_comb
     concurrent assignments, f_result_comb_proc, and everything else in
     cast256_enc stay exactly as they are.


2. DUAL-CHANNEL GCTR PIPELINING IN gcm.vhd  [WORKING - KEEP]

   When j0_ready_r='1' and pt_blk_rem_r > 1, GCTR_ENC launches channel 1
   on CB_n+1 in parallel with channel 0 on CB_n (pair_mode_r='1').
   New states GCM_GCTR_WAIT2 and GCM_GCTR_LAST2 process the second block.
   Logically correct. No timing issues from this change alone.


3. AAD DMA-GHASH OVERLAP IN gcm.vhd  [WORKING - KEEP]

   The DMA read for AAD block N+1 is issued while GHASH processes block N.
   Signal aad_dma_inflight_r guards the exit from GCM_AAD_HASH to prevent
   skipping the last AAD block (race bug found and fixed in commit 2fab746).
   Logically correct.


4. TRIPLE-CHANNEL CAST-256  [LOGICALLY CORRECT - KEEP, BUT WORSENS TIMING]

   cast256.vhd: N_ENG changed from 2 to 3. New ch2 ports added.
   gcm.vhd: triple_mode_r signal, GCM_GCTR_WAIT3, GCM_GCTR_LAST3 states.
   When j0_ready_r='1' and pt_blk_rem_r > 2, all three channels encrypt
   CB_n, CB_n+1, CB_n+2 simultaneously.

   The third channel adds ~2,000 LUTs, pushing utilisation to ~83% and
   worsening routing congestion. It compounds the forwarding timing violation
   (from -3.7 ns with 2 channels to -9.4 ns with 3 channels) but is not
   itself the root cause. Once forwarding is reverted, 3 channels with
   ADDR→COMPUTE should meet timing (the design was at ~65% LUT with 2
   channels and met timing; 83% with 3 channels and ADDR→COMPUTE should
   still be within reach - needs verification by re-running synthesis).


5. 32-BIT GHASH UNROLL IN gf128_mul.vhd  [WORKING - KEEP]

   Loop unrolled from 16 to 32 iterations per cycle. Counter narrowed from
   3-bit (0..7) to 2-bit (0..3). GF multiplication latency: 9 → 5 cycles.
   GHASH block latency: 10 → 6 cycles. Critical path ~64 LUT levels ≈ 6–7 ns,
   within the 10 ns budget. This change is independent of the forwarding
   problem and is not part of the critical path.
